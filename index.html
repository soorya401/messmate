UX Case Study — Food Ordering

MessMate

Redesigning how mess-hall regulars in a college town pre-order meals, so the line at the counter disappears and the kitchen stops guessing portions.

Role — UX & UI Design Timeline — 3 weeks Tools — Figma, user interviews, HTML/CSS/JS prototype
01 — The Problem

Everyone orders the same lunch, at the same window, at the same minute.

Mess halls near college campuses hit a hard crunch between 12:30 and 1:00 PM. Students queue for a menu they can't see until they reach the counter, the kitchen over-cooks "safe" items to avoid running out, and anyone with a short break skips lunch entirely rather than wait.

The brief: design a lightweight pre-ordering flow that removes the guesswork for both sides of the counter, without asking students to adopt a whole new habit.

02 — Research

Three insights from talking to 11 students and 2 mess owners.

INSIGHT 01

Students decide what to eat while still in class, not while standing in line — the ordering moment needs to happen 20–40 minutes before they arrive, not at the counter.

INSIGHT 02

Mess owners don't want a dashboard to learn — they wanted totals shouted out or printed, since the kitchen runs on paper tickets, not screens.

INSIGHT 03

Nobody wanted an account or a login. Every extra step before ordering was named, unprompted, as a reason people would just skip the app and queue as usual.

06 — Reflection

What I'd test next.

The prototype assumes students trust a printed ticket number enough to skip the line — that's untested at scale. Next iteration: run it for one real lunch service with a paper sign, measure how many people actually walk past the queue, and interview the two who don't.
